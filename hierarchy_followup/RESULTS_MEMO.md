## 1. Summary

The memo asked whether hierarchical structuring pays where nature uses it (flaw tolerance, crack deflection, graceful failure) and at a scale where the levels are actually separated. The answer, in this model and at equal mass:

| test | question | verdict against the pre-registered criterion | what was found |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 crack arrest (120 Å, level ratio 3) | do veins arrest a crack? | FAIL (retention 0.98–1.06 × rule, criterion ≥ 1.15) | **notch insensitivity**: a crack inside a domain costs the veins mesh exactly its cross-section (retention 0.90/0.97) while the one-level mesh (0.73/0.74), the slit array (0.56–0.61) and the pristine sheet (0.49–0.58) lose far more; a crack that severs a vein removes the advantage |
| T4 two-stage failure (120 Å) | can wide veins carry load after the fine level fails? | FAIL | veins carry 0.84 (W16) or 0.57 (W20) of the peak after the first avalanche but only for 0.02–0.07 strain; post-peak energy ≈ the one-level control |
| T2 Cook–Gordon seams (120 Å) | do weak rows ahead of a crack deflect it? | FAIL | work-to-failure ratios 0.88–1.07 for every seam spacing (criterion 1.3); strength follows the seam porosity; failure becomes progressive without energy gain |
| T3 scale separation (300 Å, level ratio 8) | does hierarchy pay when the levels are separated? | **PASS (work to failure)**, plus an unexpected strength premium | two-level 19.3/19.3 N/m and 1.9/2.4 J/m² against 15.3 and 15.5 N/m, 1.1–1.5 J/m² for both one-level designs of the same mass; with a 60 Å crack 15.5/14.7 N/m against 11.0–11.6: the cracked hierarchical sheet is as strong as the intact one-level sheets |
| T5 composite hierarchies (300 Å) | do levels of *different* design help? | not passed on the residual clause; energy and crack clauses met by fibrous veins and grids; porous-vein null confirmed | strength follows the column rule for every composite (no composite beats solid veins); **fibrous veins keep their full work to failure under a 60 Å crack** and fail gracefully over 0.15–0.20 strain; **three levels give the largest post-peak energy** (0.8–1.3 J/m²) at the two-level strength; porous veins are no barrier (retention 0.65) |

One sentence for the abstract: *In this model hierarchy buys load-path alignment and, once the levels are separated by a factor of about eight, strength, work to failure and notch insensitivity at equal mass; it never beats the net-section rule under a crack, and what it buys beyond strength depends on the building blocks: solid veins for strength, fibre-bundle veins for crack-tolerant toughness, sub-veins for graceful failure.*

## 3. Results by test

### T1 — crack arrest by veins (120 Å cell, 22 runs)

Designs: pristine sheet, one-level elongated-pore mesh, two-level veins mesh (the phase-1 best nested design: 12 Å veins every 40 Å, elongated fine pores), load-parallel slit array; each with a 20 Å and a 40 Å crack perpendicular to the load, two registries, plus the uncracked references in the same cell.

| design | σ intact (N/m) | 20 Å crack: retention (rule) | 40 Å crack: retention (rule) | W ratio at 20 Å |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pristine sheet | 38.8 | 0.58 / 0.49 (0.83 / 0.85) | 0.45 / 0.44 (0.68) | 0.20 / 0.14 |
| one-level ellipse mesh | 18.4 / 18.4 | 0.73 / 0.74 (0.88 / 0.85) | 0.61 / 0.62 (0.70 / 0.67) | 0.46 / 0.56 |
| two-level veins mesh | 19.6 / 19.6 | **0.90 / 0.97** (0.92 / 0.91) | 0.64 / 0.59 (0.70 / 0.70) | **0.90 / 0.94** |
| slit array | 25.4 / 25.4 | 0.61 / 0.56 (0.92 / 0.94) | 0.56 / 0.59 (0.74 / 0.72) | 0.54 / 0.35 |

Mechanism (panels in `figures/hierarchy/panels/`): with the crack inside a domain, the load-parallel veins carry the load around it; the first bonds break in other domains and the crack does not move before the peak (no vein bond broken before the peak in either registry). The crack costs exactly its section, whereas every other design loses 12–40 % more than its section. The 40 Å crack severs a vein and the advantage disappears. The slit array is the surprise of T1: its strips are coupled by the bridges between slit tips, so one severed strip takes its neighbours with it (retention 0.56–0.61 against a rule of 0.92).

Verdict: the "≥ 1.15 × rule" clause fails (0.98 and 1.06), the "≥ 1.10 × control" clause passes (1.23 and 1.31). Hierarchy at this scale buys insensitivity to a contained flaw, not a bonus above the net section.

Registry: sharp cracks in pristine graphene are registry-sensitive (22.4 vs 19.0 N/m for the same 20 Å crack shifted by half a lattice constant, lattice trapping); uncracked meshes agree to 0.1 N/m; cracked meshes to 0.2–1.4 N/m.

### T4 — designed two-stage failure (120 Å, 4 runs)

Veins 16 and 20 Å every 40 Å along the load (vein fractions 0.4 and 0.5), round fine pores, two registries.

| design | σ (rule) | residual after the first avalanche (rule) | post-peak energy (control 0.21 J/m²) | post-avalanche strain range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W16 | 18.0 / 18.0 (18.2) | 0.84 / 0.84 (0.63) | 0.22 / 0.19 | 0.025 / 0.020 |
| W20 | 17.8 / 17.8 (19.5) | 0.57 / 0.58 (0.74) | 0.29 / 0.30 | 0.070 / 0.075 |

The strengths sit on the column rule. The veins survive the fine level's failure only briefly: the crack-tip fields of the failed domains cut them within 0.02–0.07 strain. Both registries agree. Verdict FAIL. (T3 and T5 show that the same design at level ratio 8 does hold a second stage, in one of two registries for solid veins and in both for fibrous veins.)

### T2 — Cook–Gordon seams (120 Å, 12 runs)

Pristine sheet with a 20 Å crack and rows of 4 Å pores along the load every 20, 30 or 40 Å (one row through the crack centre, pores flanking the crack plane), a weaker seam (pitch 6.5 Å), and two controls (the same pores in columns across the load; random placement); two registries.

| design | W ratio to the cracked pristine sheet (rule, hypothesis) | σ ratio | deflection seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| seams every 20 Å | 0.88 / 0.94 (0.93, 1.3) | 0.73 / 0.79 | r0 yes, r1 no |
| seams every 30 Å | 1.07 / 0.97 (0.95, 1.3) | 0.92 / 0.92 | r0 yes, r1 no |
| seams every 40 Å | 1.02 / 1.07 (0.96, 1.3) | 0.94 / 0.92 | no |
| weak seam every 20 Å | 0.97 / 1.06 (0.91, 1.3) | 0.67 / 0.75 | r0 yes, r1 no |
| columns across the load (control) | 0.60 / 1.28 | 0.53 / 0.63 | fails at the perforated columns (11.8 N/m, alternative rule 11.9) |
| random pores (control) | 0.72 / 0.90 | 0.64 / 0.68 | |

No toughening: the work-to-failure ratio equals the rule (1 − seam porosity) within scatter for every spacing; seams at 20 Å change the failure from one avalanche to a progressive one without energy gain. The Cook–Gordon condition (interface strength well below a fifth of the cohesive strength) is not met by rows of pores in graphene even at the weaker pitch. Verdict FAIL.

### T3 — scale separation (300 Å, level ratio 8, 11 runs)

| design | σ r0 / r1 (N/m) | column rule | phase-1 alignment trend | W (J/m²) | 60 Å crack: σ, retention (rule) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| two-level: 30 Å veins every 100 Å, elongated fine pores | **19.3 / 19.3** | 17.5 | 22.1 | **1.91 / 2.35** | **15.5 / 14.7**, 0.80 / 0.76 (0.85 / 0.84) |
| one-level fine (elongated pores, period 12 Å) | 15.3 / 15.3 | 13.9 | 18.4 | 1.42 / 1.54 | 11.0 / 11.6, 0.72 / 0.76 (0.82) |
| one-level coarse (50 Å bars) | 15.5 / 15.5 | 16.0 | 11.6 | 1.15 / 1.12 | 11.0, 0.71 (0.67, one bar severed) |

The two-level design is 25 % stronger and 35–65 % tougher than both one-level designs of the same mass; the strength premium (+3.8 to +4.0 N/m) and the work premium (+0.5 to +1.2 J/m²) are far outside the one-level scatter (1.9 N/m, 0.31 J/m²) and identical in both registries. The pre-registered hypothesis ("a work or crack-retention premium although the strength shows none") is exceeded on work; the strength premium was not predicted by the memo but is largely anticipated by the column class rule (veins are wide straight ligaments, the strongest class). Verdict PASS.

Mechanism (`figures/hierarchy/mechanism_T3.png`, panels): before the peak the veins carry 41 % of the load on 30 % of the section; the fine level fails compartment by compartment, each domain crack stopping at the veins above and below (stepwise decline), and the veins break last. In the fine one-level mesh a crack runs through row after row with nothing to stop it; in the coarse mesh the three bars are equally loaded and fail in one avalanche of 327 bonds. With a 60 Å crack (eight pore rows), the two-level design holds the crack at the veins until the peak and then deflects it into two branches per tip (a diamond-shaped damage zone about 100 Å wide); its cracked strength equals the intact one-level strengths. Its retention is 0.91–0.94 of the section rule, so, as in T1, there is no arrest bonus above the rule.

Two findings about the rules: the phase-1 alignment trend (fitted on 120 Å cells) over-predicts every 300 Å design by 3–4 N/m and does not separate the aligned fine mesh from the isotropic coarse mesh (15.3 vs 15.5 despite A = 0.32 vs 0.02), whereas the column class rule stays within 2 N/m of all three; and the aligned designs lose 3.6–4 N/m from 120 to 300 Å (weakest-link size effect on pore-edge ligaments) while the coarse mesh loses 1.5.

Levels made of different designs, all mass-matched to 0.20 (except the solid-bar control at 0.12), generated by `atomistics/structures/composite.py`.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| aligned-pore-mesh veins + round pores | 14.8 / 14.6 (13.4; 15.1 with the mesh's own class) | 1.95 / 2.09 | 0.69 / 0.87, 0.20 | 0.66 (0.84), 0.52 |
| round-pore-mesh veins + elongated pores | 15.0 / 15.0 (13.7) | 1.79 / 1.91 | 0.70 / 0.82, 0.20 | 0.65 (0.82), 0.50 |
| mesh of meshes (grid, round-pore bars) | 11.1 / 11.4 (9.5) | 0.96 / 0.99 | 0.20 / 0.30, 0.075 / 0.055 | 0.75 / 0.82 (0.83 / 0.80), 0.76 |
| fibre-bar frame (slit-bundle bars along the load) | 15.8 / 15.6 (17.6) | 1.32 / 1.22 | 0.33 / 0.27, 0.045 / 0.030 | 0.84 (0.88), **1.47** |
| three levels (veins, 8 Å sub-veins every 33 Å, elongated pores) | 18.6 / 19.7 (17.8) | 2.67 / 2.47 | **1.26 / 0.77**, 0.20 / 0.20 | 0.80 (0.82), 0.84 |
| solid-bar grid, φ 0.12 (control) | 18.4 / 18.4 (18.8) | 1.28 / 1.30 | 0.05, single avalanche | 0.60 (0.80), 0.64 |

Findings:
1. Strength follows the column class rule for every composite (within 1–2 N/m, the aligned-mesh veins on the variant that assigns that mesh its own net-section strength). No composite beats solid veins in strength; the rule says none can.
2. Toughness depends on the building blocks. Post-peak energy ranks: three levels (0.8–1.3 J/m²) > fibrous veins (0.8–0.9, both registries) ≈ porous veins (0.7–0.9) > solid veins (0.3–1.0, registry-dependent) > grids (0.2–0.3) > solid bars (0.05). Sub-veins and fibre bundles multiply the number of arrest lines or of load paths that fail separately.
3. Crack tolerance needs continuous strips. Solid and fibrous veins, sub-veined domains and the fibre-bar frame sit on the section rule under a crack (0.80–0.87); porous veins fall to 0.65; solid bars to 0.60. Only the bundle designs keep or increase their work to failure under a crack (1.0–1.5).
4. The solid-vein second stage at scale is registry-dependent (0.96 vs 0.28 J/m²), the fibrous-vein one is not.

Against the pre-registered clauses: the "residual ≥ 0.6 in both registries" clause fails for every fibrous design (0.46–0.74), the "≥ 1.15 × rule" clause fails everywhere (the rule is met, not beaten), the energy clauses pass for fibrous veins and both grids, the porous-vein null hypothesis is confirmed, and the three-level strength clause passes (within the two-level scatter) while its crack clause does not (0.80 vs 0.80 for two levels).

## 4. Cross-cutting findings

- **The net-section class rule is the rule that survives.** Across 78 runs the column rule (section × ligament class, seam-free sections) predicts strength to a median relative error of about 10 %; the phase-1 alignment index does not transfer to 300 Å. Under a crack, no design of any test exceeded the net-section rule by the 15 % the memo hoped for; hierarchy's benefit is to *reach* the rule where one-level designs fall below it.
- **Scale is the hidden variable of the phase-1 hierarchy result.** At level ratio 3 (phase 1, T1, T4) hierarchy adds nothing to strength or toughness; at level ratio 8 (T3, T5) it adds 25 % strength and 35–65 % toughness at equal mass. The phase-1 statement "hierarchy is not a free lunch" is correct only with the qualifier "at level ratios up to 3".
- **Registry matters where there is a sharp tip or a marginal barrier**: 3.4 N/m for a crack in pristine graphene, 0.1 N/m for uncracked meshes, and the difference between a long and a short second stage for solid veins at 300 Å. Two registries were the minimum; three would be better for the solid-vein designs.
- **Standard prediction scores** (`evaluate_predictions`, all 78 runs): modulus median relative error 3 %, work to failure 15 %, failure strain 13 %; fracture-mode class accuracy 41 % (the rules mostly predicted "progressive" where "stepwise" occurred).

## 5. Corrections to the phase-1 record (found while preparing this campaign)

1. `S6_mesh_p16_offset3` duplicates `offset1`: the registry vector (1.23, 2.13) Å is a primitive translation of the honeycomb lattice (ASSESSMENT_phase1.md §7). Phase 1 therefore has two, not three, registries of the reference mesh.
2. `min_solid_fraction_across_x` carries a raster-seam artefact in every 120 Å cell (stored 0.72–0.80 where the real minimum column is 0.85–0.88; §9). The paper's Fig. 2 predictor and class medians used the contaminated values; the bias largely cancels within a cell size but not between 100 and 120 Å cells. Phase 3 uses seam-free sections and recomputed class medians (straight 28.9, coarse round 29.4, fine round 16.6, random 19.5 N/m).
3. 27 of the 114 discovery and sweep runs (24 %) ended on the 0.12 post-peak strain bound with load still carried; their work-to-failure and post-peak metrics are lower bounds. The bound itself is not documented anywhere in the phase-1 record; it was set with the first campaign spec after the unbounded convergence runs.

## 7. Limitations and next steps

- Model results only (screened REBO2, athermal, 2D periodic cells); work to failure is an area under a curve, not a toughness; no crack-length scaling was attempted.
- 300 Å cells hold three domains; the weakest-link size effect seen between 120 and 300 Å has not been followed further. A three-level design with proper separation at every level needs about 600 Å (≈ 110,000 atoms, 13 GB of GPU memory, 10–20 h per run at the measured speed).
- Three registries for the solid-vein round-pore design would settle whether its second stage is typical.
- The composite generator can nest any of slits, round or elongated pores, solid graphene or sub-veins inside veins or a square grid; the design space is much larger than the eight archetypes run here, and the column rule now provides a cheap screen for it.
