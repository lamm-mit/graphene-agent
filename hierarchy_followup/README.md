# Hierarchy follow-up (phase 3): does hierarchical structuring pay where nature uses it?

A separate tree for the follow-up campaign planned in `HIERARCHY_FOLLOWUP_MEMO.md` (T1 crack arrest by veins, T2
Cook–Gordon seams, T3 scale separation, T4 designed two-stage failure). It contains a copy of the phase-1 platform
code (force engine, loading driver, generators, descriptors, database, queue) plus the phase-3 additions, and it writes
**all new results here** (`experiments/database/`, `trajectories/`, `results/`, `figures/`). The phase-1/2 tree
`../carbon_discovery/` is never written to; its 132 records are read from the copies under `reference/phase1_runs/`
(trajectories of phase-1 runs are read from `../carbon_discovery/trajectories/` when a figure needs them).

*different* designs: fibrous veins made of slit bundles with elongated-pore or round-pore domains (VF_E, VF_R), veins made of the
aligned elongated-pore mesh or of a round-pore mesh (VE_R, VR_E), solid veins with round-pore domains (VS_R, the solid-vein reference),
a mesh of meshes (GR_0: square grid whose bars are a round-pore mesh), a fibre-bar frame (GFx_0), a three-level design (V3_E: veins,
sub-veins, elongated pores) and a solid-bar grid control (GS, porosity 0.12, not mass-matched). Its own prediction file
`hierarchy_T5_predictions.json` and queue `experiments/specs/hierarchy_T5/` (`scripts/run_T5.sh` waits for queue 2).

Rules and success criteria are in the docstrings of `experiments/campaign_hierarchy_followup.py` and `experiments/campaign_T5_composites.py`
and in the `notes` of the prediction files. The curve metrics of T4 (first avalanche = first single-step stress drop > 15 % of the peak; residual =
load right after it; second peak = largest local maximum after it; post-peak energy and strain range) are defined once in
`analysis/assess_phase1.curve_metrics` and used unchanged for phase-1 references and new runs.

## Deviations from the memo (and why)

1. **Registry replicates.** The memo's registry vector (1.23, 2.13) Å is a primitive translation of the honeycomb
   lattice, so it reproduces the unshifted structure exactly (phase 1's `S6_mesh_p16_offset3` equals `offset1` to two
   decimals for that reason, see `ASSESSMENT_phase1.md` §7). The follow-up shifts the whole pattern rigidly by (1.23, 0) Å,
   which is not a lattice translation (`tests/test_followup.py`); for that the fine lattice of `nanomesh_hier` and the
   `slit_array` got an `offset` key (defaults reproduce phase 1).
2. **All of T1 in one cell (120 Å) with both registries of every uncracked reference.** The memo counted 16 runs by
   reusing phase-1 records made in 100 Å cells; the 30 Å crack moves by 4 % between 100 and 150 Å cells, so every
   retention ratio here is formed within one cell and one registry. The pristine reference gets both crack lengths and
   both registries (4 short runs).
3. **Crack placement.** Veins mesh: the 20 Å crack is centred mid-domain (tips 4 Å from the vein edges, as in the memo);
   the 40 Å crack is centred on a vein, which it severs, with its tips 14 Å from the next veins (the memo's mid-domain
   placement would end both tips on vein centre lines). Slit array: the crack is centred on a strip so that it severs
   whole strips (one for 20 Å, three for 40 Å) and nothing else.
4. **T4 fine level uses round pores** (memo: elongated pores along the load). At the fine-level porosities T4 needs
   inside the domains (0.33 and 0.40) elongated pores at period 12 merge into slits (tip ligaments < 2 Å); and round-pore
   fine levels fail at 0.12–0.13 strain against 0.15–0.19 for aligned ellipses and ~0.20 for straight veins, which gives
   the two stages the largest strain separation, i.e. the hypothesis its best chance.
5. **T2 in the 120 Å cell** (memo: 100 Å) so that the memo's row spacings 20/30/40 Å divide the cell exactly; rows are
   anchored at the crack centre (tips 10/20/30 Å from the next rows), pores flank the crack plane (a ligament, not a pore,
   lies on the crack path), and the two pores that would touch the crack flanks are omitted. One extra design
   (`weakseamX`, pitch 6.5 Å instead of 8 Å) tests a weaker seam because the Cook–Gordon condition (interface strength
   below about a fifth of the cohesive strength) is only marginally met by the memo's seam. Every T2 design has two
   registries from the start (the memo planned registries for the best spacing afterwards).
6. **T3 cracked controls.** The memo's cracked coarse one-level mesh is kept (a 60 Å crack at the cell centre severs one
   of the three 50 Å bars, so its rule is a 2/3 retention that no column count can see), and the fine one-level mesh with
   the same 60 Å crack is added as the proper "no barrier" control of the cracked two-level design. Both registries are run
   for T3 except for the cracked coarse mesh.
7. **Loading protocol.** Every physics constant of phase 1 is unchanged (strain steps 1 % / 0.5 % bisected to 0.125 %,
   FIRE 0.02 eV/Å, transverse relaxation); the termination bounds are extended (`max_strain` 0.32 → 0.35,
   `post_peak_strain_limit` 0.12 → 0.20) so that a second load-carrying stage cannot be cut off (six phase-1 nested tails
   were).
8. **Porosity matching tolerance** 0.005 instead of 0.008.
9. **Sections are measured with the raster seam excluded.** The phase-1 descriptor `min_solid_fraction_across_x` takes its
   minimum over a 0.5 Å occupancy raster that is up to one column longer than the cell; for all 120 Å cells the minimum is
   that seam column (e.g. 0.72 against 0.85 for a real column of the ellipse mesh), see `ASSESSMENT_phase1.md` §9. The
   follow-up's rules use `atomistics/descriptors/columns.py` (interior columns only, crack column for cracked designs) and the
   ligament-class medians recomputed with seam-free sections (straight 28.9, coarse round 29.4, fine round
   16.5, random 19.5 N/m; with the stored sections the paper's 30.3 / 29.8 / 16.9 / 19.9). The first
   prediction file (v1, 13:52:58) had divided the crack-column section by the seam value; it is kept as
   `hierarchy_followup_predictions_v1_superseded.json` and was replaced by v2 at 14:04:20 (status log).

## What the phase-1 database already says (summary of `ASSESSMENT_phase1.md`)

Nested meshes sit 1.6 ± 1.4 N/m *below* the one-level trend at equal alignment (reproduced exactly); their post-peak
behaviour is a staircase decline after the first avalanche, not a second peak (best nested design: 1.00 → 0.72 → 0.61 →
0.47 → 0.25 of the peak over four steps); the square-vein W16/W20 designs of phase 1 did not keep their veins after the
fine level failed; the pristine sheet loses half its strength to a 20 Å crack while porous meshes cannot amplify a tip that
way. Rule inputs: ligament-class net-section strengths with seam-free sections (straight 28.9, coarse round
29.4, fine round 16.5, random 19.5 N/m) and the one-level trend σ = 16.8·A + 13.2 N/m (scatter
1.9 N/m). Two findings about the phase-1 record itself: one of its three registry replicates is a lattice translation
(§7), and its minimum-section descriptor carries a raster-seam artefact in 120 Å cells (§9).

## Ground rules kept from phase 1

Mechanics only from the screened REBO2 engine (validated 20/20; the 10 platform tests pass in this tree with Atomistica
1.2.7); 2D stress in N/m, work to failure is an area under the curve; model results only. Never glob-delete inside
`experiments/database/`. Never write into `../carbon_discovery/`. New designs get new names (`H1_`…`H4_`); records are
never overwritten. Differences below 1 N/m are noise (float32 MPS reproducibility 0.8 %; registry scatter).
